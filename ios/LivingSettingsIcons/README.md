# LivingSettingsIcons — Telegram-iOS module

Settings icons that come alive. When you come back from a row's page, its icon tells you where you were and every part
of it moves: the bell rings while its clapper swings and its badge swells, the padlock's shackle springs open and slams
shut, the person in My Profile ducks and pops back up, the arrows run through the Proxy shield, and so on. The tile pops
on a spring and a sheen crosses it. Then it is the same still icon again. Tapping a row squashes its icon under the
finger.

The web prototype is the reference: `prototype/src/settings/living-icons.js` (the rigs, the cuts, effects and player)
and `prototype/src/settings/nav.js` (when an icon plays). Every Swift file names the prototype code it mirrors. Web and
native draw the same thing: Telegram's own glyphs and tiles, cut into the same parts, moved by the same rigs.

The drop-in module goes in `submodules/TelegramUI/Components/PeerInfo/LivingSettingsIcons`. The module, its glue and the
hooks in `PeerInfoScreen` are all in `ios/patches/0003-living-icons.patch`, made against Telegram-iOS `f1dd7a2`:

```sh
git apply /path/to/tgdesign/ios/patches/0003-living-icons.patch       # alone, or in order with the other patches
```

`ios/LivingSettingsIcons` is the single source of truth. To rebuild the patch from it, run
`python3 tools/living-icons-patch.py > ios/patches/0003-living-icons.patch`. The script fetches the pristine files at
`f1dd7a2`, or reads them from `PRISTINE_DIR`.

| `Sources/LivingIconRig.swift` | `RIGS` shape, `P[name].set/dash`, `fx.*`, `E.*` | `LivingIconPartTransform` holds `{ r, x, y, sx, sy, kx, o, cx, cy }` and its exact SVG matrix. `LivingIconFrame` is what `run(t, P, fx, E)` asks for in one frame. `LivingIconRig` has the duration, flags and `run`. |
| `Sources/LivingIconRigs.swift` | `RIGS[name].run` | Every rig: bell, phone, folder, saved, data, devices, lock, profile, proxy, stars, premium, power, language, gift, business, ask, faq, features, appearance, money, set-emoji-status, set-avatar, set-covercolor. | `Sources/LivingIconEffects.swift` | `play()`'s `fx`, `update()`, `press()` | The effects as pure functions of time: waves, glow, glint, halo, sparkles, confetti, the typing dots, rays, paint stroke, paper, flash, hue (the CSS `hue-rotate`/`saturate` matrices), spin3d, the turning globe and its cross-fade. Also the tile's spring pop, the sheen and the press. |
| `Sources/LivingIconArtwork.swift` | `RIGS[name].parts` / `.build(c)`, `play()`; `CUTS`, `STAR_WEDGES` | Every icon as a part tree: groups, clips, cut-outs whose holes move, the paper, the bubble's dots, the turning globe. `parts()` cuts a file by subpath index exactly as `play()` does; `tree(name)` is each rig's cut, line for line. `Cuts` and `TurningGlobe` are where Telegram's glyphs are cut. Also `LOCK_*`, `PROFILE_BUST`, `bookmark(n, f, g)`. |
| `Sources/LivingIconArtworkFiles.swift` | `prototype/assets/icons/*.svg` | The glyph path data (Telegram's glyphs at `f1dd7a2`). Generated by `tools/living-icons-art.mjs`. |

| `Sources/LivingSettingsIcon.swift` | `settings-screen.js` rows, `SettingsColors`, `SettingsTiles` | The public `LivingSettingsIcon` (one case per row) and `LivingSettingsIconTile`. The tile, its rounded clip and the sheen pop together, and particles live in an unclipped layer around the tile. |
| `Sources/LivingSettingsIconPlayer.swift` | `LivingIcons` | `play(_:imageNode:container:glyphColor:cornerRadius:)`, `press(imageNode:container:)` and `stop()`. One icon at a time, on `SharedDisplayLinkDriver` (`.max`) only while something moves. |

## Artwork: Telegram's glyph at rest

During the animation the row's static icon (its `ASImageNode`) is hidden and the layers draw the icon instead, so at
rest the layers must be exactly Telegram's icon:

- **The tile** is rendered with Telegram's own code path (`LivingSettingsIconTile`). The prototype draws the same
  tiles: `renderSettingsIcon`'s gradient from its first colour at the top-right to the bottom-left (Business
  `#A95CE3 → #F16B80`), the Premium, Stars and Gift tiles' own gradients from the bottom-left, Features in `#ffcc02`.
- **The glyph** is Telegram's own (`prototype/assets/icons` holds the `f1dd7a2` assets as SVG; lock, My Profile and the
  bookmark are rebuilt there as parts), cut into parts that cover it exactly at rest. Web and native cut it the same way
  (`tree(name)` mirrors each rig's `parts` / `build`), and CI draws every icon at rest with CoreGraphics against
  Telegram's glyph (`RestTests`).
- **Money** is Telegram's bitmap, so it turns as a whole (`spin3d`). Its sparkle is split out of the bitmap to turn and
  flare on its own — natively by `LivingIconImageSplit`, in the prototype as two images split the same way.

Where one of Telegram's outlines holds parts that move apart, it is cut (living-icons.js `CUTS`):

| Row | Telegram's glyph | Parts |
|---|---|---|
| Telegram Features | lamp | the bulb and the base; the highlight is drawn on in the tile's colour |
| Send a Gift | box, lid with the bow | box; the lid and the bow parted at y 9, the lid reaching 0.25 pt into the bow (no seam) |
| Ask a Question | bubble with three dots cut out | the bubble; its own dots bounce in turn (`fx.dots`) |
| Telegram FAQ | disc with the question mark cut out | the mark is undrawn and redrawn along its centre line (a 3.2 pt stroke, clipped to the mark), wobbles; the dot hops |
| Telegram Premium | rounded star with a swoosh | a core and five arms cut by wedges at its concave corners (`STAR_WEDGES`); the arms fly out |
| Power Saving | battery | the shell (still) and its charge (`top`), which jolts inside it |
| Telegram Business | shop | the awning (`lid`) and the shop with its window (`base`) |
| Appearance | ring with its left half filled | the ring (`handle`) and the half disc (`tip`), which stays inside the ring's opening (r 8.64) as it trails |
| Language | banded globe | cross-fades in 0.06 s into the same globe drawn as a disc cut by its latitudes and two moving meridians, and back over the last 0.1 s |

## Hooks in PeerInfoScreen (patch 0003)

The hunks are small and avoid the lines the other patches change:

- `PeerInfoScreen/BUILD` gets one dependency, `//submodules/TelegramUI/Components/PeerInfo/LivingSettingsIcons`.
- `PeerInfoScreenNode` gets `lazy var livingIcons = PeerInfoSettingsLivingIcons()`.
- `PeerInfoScreenImpl` gets a new `viewWillAppear(_:)` that calls `livingIconsWillAppear(animated:)`, one line in
  `viewWillDisappear` (`livingIconsWillDisappear()`) and one in `viewDidAppear` (`livingIconsDidAppear()`).
- `openSettings(section:)` (`PeerInfoScreenSettingsActions.swift`), the one place that knows which row was opened,
  gets `self.livingIconsWillOpen(section: section)`.
- `PeerInfoScreenDisclosureItemNode` and `PeerInfoScreenActionItemNode` get an extension at the end of their files that
  exposes the row's icon node (`PeerInfoScreenLivingIconItemNode`).
- `PeerInfoSettingsLivingIcons.swift` is a new file:
  - **Finding the row.** `PeerInfoSettingsSection` maps to the row's `SettingsSection` and item id, the same ids
    `settingsItems` uses. The node is found in `regularSections[…].itemNodes[…]`.
  - **On a tap.** The icon is pressed, and the row is remembered.
  - **When Settings goes away.** If this happens within 2 s of the tap, the row's page was pushed, so the node waits
    for Settings to come back. Otherwise it forgets the row.
  - **On a back-button pop.** `NavigationContainer` calls `viewWillAppear(true)` when the pop starts. The icon plays
    0.2 s later, when the 0.5 s spring is at 95 %, as the prototype does.
  - **On a swipe back.** `viewWillAppear(true)` arrives as the finger starts. A pan in progress up the view chain shows
    it is a swipe, so nothing is scheduled. When the swipe completes, `viewWillAppear(false)` and `viewDidAppear(false)`
    arrive and the icon plays then. If the swipe is cancelled, nothing plays.
  - **Fallback.** `viewDidAppear` plays the icon if it has not played yet.
  - **Where it plays.** Only on screen, over the row's card, with particles clipped to the card's rounded rect (r 26,
    `cornersImage(glass:)`).
  - The emoji status row is left out: it opens a picker over Settings, not a page, and its icon depends on the status.

## Lifecycle and performance

- **At rest nothing exists.** There is no layer and no display link, and the static icon shows as before.
- **During an icon (about 1 s).** One `LivingSettingsIconLayer` is a few dozen small vector layers. One
  `SharedDisplayLinkDriver` link applies the rig's frame without implicit actions. Paths are rebuilt only for the
  bookmark, the paper, the flash and the globe's meridians. On the frame at or past the duration, the layer is removed
  and the static icon shown again.
- **One icon at a time.** Playing another icon stops the current one. A press and a playing icon can overlap, as in
  the prototype.
- **Cost.** The tile image is drawn once per palette and Money's split is computed once.

## Accessibility

With Reduce Motion (`UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled`), nothing presses and nothing plays: the icons stay still,
exactly as today. VoiceOver is unaffected, because the rows' accessibility areas are not touched.

## Verification

`ios/ci/verify-all.sh` (any Mac CI) and `.github/workflows/ios-living-icons.yml` check three things:

1. **`Standalone/LivingIconsCore`** (`swift test`) checks the pure Swift (rigs, effects, artwork, path parser, image
   split) against the web:
   - **Rig parity.** `tools/golden-icons.mjs` imports the prototype's `RIGS` and plays its own `LivingIcons` player
     against a minimal fake DOM, recording at 60 fps for every rig:
     - each part's `set(...)`, `dash`, `E.*` change and `fx.*` call;
     - every value the player's effects write: wave arcs, sparkles, confetti, the typing dots, rays, stroke, paper,
       flash, halo, glow/hue filters, spin3d, the turning globe's meridians and cross-fade, tile pop and sheen;
     - the press.

     The Swift replays all of it within 1e-6. `toFixed` is widened while recording, so DOM rounding does not hide any
     drift.
   - **Path parser.** It is checked against the piece bounds of an independent reader.
   - **Cuts.** `parts()` is checked against `play()`'s tree node for node; for every rig, what each part holds (its
     shapes' bounds, cut by their clips) is checked against what the prototype's part holds; the constants of the cuts
     are the prototype's `CUTS` and `STAR_WEDGES`.
   - **At rest.** Every icon at rest is checked against Telegram's glyph (`RestTests`). Money's split is checked to
     recompose the image.
2. **`Standalone/LivingIconsTypeCheck`** builds every module source for the iOS Simulator with `-warnings-as-errors`.
   It uses signature stubs copied from Telegram's AsyncDisplayKit, Display (`SimpleLayer` family, `ASImageNode`,
   `SharedDisplayLinkDriver`, `generateImage`, `deviceColorSpace`) and AppBundle.
3. **The patch** applies to the pristine `f1dd7a2` files, alone and in order with the other patches.

**What CI does not check:**

- **The `PeerInfoScreen` glue and hooks.** They need the whole Telegram-iOS Bazel build. They are small, follow the
  surrounding code, and were checked against the sources at `f1dd7a2`. The real proof is building the patched app.
- **The layers on screen.** The layer tree's maths is tested. Its rendering by Core Animation, and the timing of the
  appearance callbacks, need the app on a device.

## Notes

- **The press** runs at the tap, while the row's page slides in. In the prototype the page is pushed 0.06 s after the
  press; in Telegram the push is immediate.
- **Rows that do not push a page** (Avatar's media picker, alerts) play only if Settings gets the appearance callbacks
  when they close.
- **Power Saving, Appearance and Business** move Telegram's battery, dial and shop with motions first written for a
  bolt, a brush and a briefcase (unchanged). They read naturally, but a designer may want to tune them
  (`TODO(design)`).
- **The globe's cross-fade** is exact on both sides: natively the turning globe is opaque (its gaps show the tile's
  image) and fades in over the banded one; in the prototype the two add up (`plus-lighter`) at 1 − w and w.
